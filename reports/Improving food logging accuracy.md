# Improving food logging accuracy

**Preserve what the person actually said, then calculate everything the evidence supports.** If someone logs two Lidl pastries and Plate has a piece weight for the exact product, the model only needs to recognize the product and extract “two”. Plate can do the multiplication. If someone logs a homemade meal, the most valuable missing fact might be the cooked weight of the batch or how much oil went into it. I would fix the evaluation and counted portions first, add support for measured recipes next, and use a blinded bilingual trial to decide where a stronger model or meal photos earn their cost.

## First make the score reflect what people ate

On November 21, 2026, wizard reported version `3549cdd`. Its 30-day feedback digest contained **8 parses, 7 saves, 4 corrected runs, 6 negative rating events and 0 label reads**. One run can attract multiple rating events. These counts suggest that reviewing corrections is worthwhile. They cannot establish a calorie error rate or rank models.

There is a specific problem with today's evaluation. If Plate suggests food A and the person saves food B, both identities exist in the stored run. The regression export looks up A and emits A's ID. A replay can therefore earn credit for repeating A on a corrected case. Recommendations explicitly rated “wrong food” are handled separately, by checking whether the rejected food comes back. The evaluator compares food and grams, constructs the expected items with zeroed nutrient values, and reports no calorie or protein error. Its matcher also runs without the normal repeat-logging service. [Feedback export](../src/Plate.Web/Features/Feedback/FeedbackAdmin.cs) · [Stored suggestion and save](../src/Plate.Web/Features/Feedback/ParseRunService.cs) · [Replay and scoring](../src/Plate.Web/Features/Feedback/ParseEvaluation.cs)

Keep the original suggestion, the rejected ID when known, and the final saved choice as separate fields. Score the first suggestion and the confirmed result separately. Use saved nutrient snapshots to evaluate agreement with user edits. Reserve the term **accuracy** for comparisons against independently weighed portions and a documented nutrition source. For repeat meals, replay only the preferences available before that meal. A regression fixture where A was replaced by B should give B credit and identify A as a repeated error.

## Use measured pieces, pack sizes and the label's actual units

Plate already matches Bulgarian and English food names, remembers the account's usual food and last amount, reads labels, and allows a person to change a suggestion. The live model returns estimated grams and a flag saying an amount was given. It discards the distinction between two pieces, half a pack and an explicit weight. The matcher has a count-times-serving calculation, but the live parser does not supply its `Units` field. Preserve the quantity, unit and original wording through matching. Explicit grams take precedence. Multiply a count only when the matched serving describes the same unit. A declared serving of two biscuits cannot supply the weight of one biscuit without conversion. [Live parser](../src/Plate.Web/Integrations/OpenRouterFoodParser.cs) · [Portion resolution](../src/Plate.Web/Features/Food/FoodMatcher.cs)

| Entry | What Plate could do |
| --- | --- |
| “Две закуски с лютеница и сирене от Лидл” | Match the filled pastry as one product. Its catalogue piece weight gives **2 × 90 g = 180 g**. The current recipe estimate of 233 kcal/100 g gives **about 419 kcal**. Show both sources so the exact calculation does not imply that Lidl published the energy value. [Bakery source notes](../tools/foods/lidl-bakery.md) |
| “Половин кофичка кисело мляко” | Half of the selected 400 g tub gives **200 g**. If the pack size is unknown, offer common sizes or a grams field. A previous serving of this yogurt is weaker evidence than the size of today's tub. |
| “250 ml of this drink” | For a panel declared per 100 ml, multiply by 2.5. Plate currently treats a per-100-ml read as per 100 g and displays a note. Retain the volume basis. Use a product-specific density only if the user logs mass against that panel. [Label normalization](../src/Plate.Core/Nutrition/LabelReading.cs) |

On the confirmation screen, distinguish the product match from the source of its nutrients and the source of its portion weight. The Bulgarian seed importer reads the serving weight but drops the source column that distinguishes the archived Lidl label from the recipe estimate. Preserve it on import. A current package panel can replace an estimated or outdated composition for future logs. Keep the source, date, market, raw/prepared basis, and existing logged snapshots. The EU rules explicitly distinguish per 100 g from per 100 ml and permit declarations for the food after preparation under stated conditions. For generic Bulgarian foods, compare high-use entries with the Bulgarian National Centre of Public Health and Analyses tables and confirm reuse terms before importing them. Plate's Bulgarian table collector currently reads Spider Sport and MacroScope. ## A saved batch can answer questions a photo cannot

For a frequently cooked dish, let the person save ingredients, edible weights, cooking fat and final cooked batch weight once, then weigh or enter their share. For an evenly mixed batch with 1,200 kcal retained and a cooked weight of 800 g, a 250 g serving contains **375 kcal**. If some oil was drained or food left on the plate, count the amount eaten. For a tray of chicken legs and potatoes, offer separate components because one plate can have a very different mix from the whole tray. Save new batches as revisions so last week's log keeps its original values. [FAO method for recipe composition](https://www.fao.org/4/y4705e/y4705e23.htm)

Use one skippable question when the answer could materially change kcal or protein. For “150 г ориз”, Plate’s long-grain white rice entries give **195 kcal if weighed cooked and about 548 kcal if weighed dry**. Ask “Кога претегли ориза?” and offer “Преди варене” and “След варене”. One tap resolves a difference of about 353 kcal. For a homemade salad, ask about dressing only if the entry leaves it open. Keep the person's answer with the recipe when it applies to future servings. An 81-person controlled feeding study found that next-day web and interviewer recalls captured 80% and 83% of items eaten, respectively. People especially missed additions and ingredients. This supports testing a targeted prompt. It supplies no reason to add oil automatically to every salad. [Plate’s USDA rice entries](../src/Plate.Web/Data/Seed/usda-foods.csv) · [Kirkpatrick et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24787491/)

Explore a meal photo as a quick inventory of foods or leftovers. For packaged products, use the existing camera workflow to capture the actual panel and package size. In a study of 52 standardized photographs with plates and cutlery visible, GPT-4o and another commercial model each had **35.8% mean absolute percentage error for energy** against weighed food and database calculations. Both tended to underestimate larger portions. That describes those models and images. A useful local comparison would pit photo estimates against Plate's existing usual amount, and against a food-specific portion measured once, such as “my bowl of yogurt”. [Fridolfsson et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41081011/)

A 200-photo canteen study tested that simpler baseline directly. On the foods each model correctly identified and quantified, a typical serving for that food gave a mean absolute portion error of 33–39 g across model-specific comparisons. The models scored 35–54 g on the same items. That canteen had recurring foods and portion patterns. Test whether Plate households have enough consistency for a once-weighed portion to save both time and error. [Canteen study, Table 3](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/18/21/3582)

## Prove the gain on Bulgarian and English meals

Start with a **40-meal pilot** split roughly evenly across packaged or counted foods, raw/cooked staples, homemade mixed dishes, and loosely described meals with a reference the researcher can establish. Include Bulgarian, English, mixed-script brand names and similar-looking product variants. Save the ordinary entry before showing anyone the scale readings. Independently record edible grams served minus leftovers, the exact label or composition source, recipe ingredients and final batch yield. Keep meals whose ingredients genuinely cannot be established in a separate test of whether Plate communicates uncertainty. These references estimate nutritional intake from evidence and still carry label and recipe error.

Give an experienced human logger and today's Plate the same text or photo, catalogue access and prior context, with the reference hidden. Score a candidate Plate on those same cases. Separately allow the candidate one clarification, barcode scan or recipe lookup to measure the benefit and time cost of obtaining extra information. Keep repeated packages, translations and versions of the same recipe together when splitting development and held-out cases. Forty meals can reveal failure modes. A broad claim of outperforming humans needs a larger independent sample, multiple human loggers, and uncertainty intervals on paired differences.

| Decision | Measure on the same held-out meals |
| --- | --- |
| Did the log improve? | Mean and median absolute kcal error per meal, signed kcal bias, errors in protein, carbohydrate and fat in grams, and the largest errors with their causes. Compare first suggestion and final save. |
| Where did the error start? | Missed or extra item, wrong product or preparation state, correct food missing from the candidate list, wrong selection from a good list, quantity error, or incorrect composition. Count correct “no match” choices too. |
| Did the extra step pay off? | Seconds and taps per log, fraction prompted, unanswered prompts, completion rate and change in error when someone answers. Keep Plate's existing goal of under two minutes to log a typical day. |
| Which model earns its cost? | Freeze cases and catalogue. Run the current system, matcher disabled, and each candidate on identical inputs. Track latency and cost per meal alongside paired errors. Check how often a “high confidence” choice is actually right, separately for identity and quantity. |

If the right food often misses the shortlist, compare the current name matching with bilingual aliases and semantic retrieval. If it is usually present, spend effort on selection and rejection. If the catalogue lacks it, source the food. A USDA study's embedding retrieval plus LLM selection achieved **90.7%** on one mapping benchmark and **65.4%** on another with many imperfect or absent matches. Those are food-identity outcomes on different databases. They support testing shortlist coverage separately and provide no estimate of Plate's calorie accuracy. [USDA food mapping study](https://www.ars.usda.gov/research/publications/publication/?seqNo115=429400)

**The first implementation should have two acceptance cases.** A replay of a replaced food credits the replacement. A Bulgarian entry for two known 90 g pastries resolves to 180 g, keeps the estimated nutrition identified as such, and still honors an explicit scale weight. Those cases give Plate a reliable way to measure the next change and a visible improvement that requires no extra work from the person logging.
