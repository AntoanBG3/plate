# Plate model lineup review

**November 24, 2026.** Plate’s core food logging is well balanced. Keep **Muse Ember at minimal** for parsing and **Gemini Decision 1** for matching. They led Plate’s November 21 tests for those jobs, and their measured costs were about $0.23 and $0.29 per 1,000 benchmark calls or cases, respectively. **Move weekly summaries off the Contributor tier** after a short check with GPT-6 Luna on a provider that meets the desired data-use policy. **Setup chat is the first place to test a cheaper model.** Carbon setup took about 71% of recorded spend after likely admin checks were excluded, and its introductory pricing ends November 30. Keep Flash 3.8 on labels and Carbon medium on unmatched-food estimates while testing those two jobs with known answers. ## The parser and matcher earn their place

On 62 texts and two repeats, **Muse Ember got 83.1% of texts fully right** by Plate’s strict definition. It found all expected items and met the amount criteria on all checked items. GPT-6 Luna got **76.6%** fully right at about **$0.09 per 1,000 parser calls**, versus Ember’s **$0.23**, despite matching list rates. Ember’s p95 was **3.94 s** with zero failed calls. Some benchmark labels disagreed with Plate’s single-piece rule. A schema bug in one competitor, timeouts and contention affected some comparisons. These results favor Ember for extracting foods, quantities and flags on the tested corpus. The 40-meal weighed pilot still has no results, so the size of the real-world calorie error is unresolved. [Parser benchmark](parsebench-2026-11-21.txt) · [Weighed-meal pilot](weighed-meal-pilot.md) · [Ember pricing](https://openrouter.ai/meta/muse-ember) · [Luna pricing](https://openrouter.ai/openai/gpt-6-luna)

**Gemini Decision 1 scored 98.1% at Plate’s 3-second cutoff**, with 451 ms p95 and correct rejection on all catalog absences in the test. Jev 1.14 scored **95.3%** at 261 ms p95, saving about **$0.11 per 1,000 benchmark cases**. The ranking-only result was **76.6%** on the November 21 snapshot. Ranking subsequently improved on local seeded cases, so rerun both models and ranking alone against the same current shortlist and held-out foods before drawing conclusions about the current gap. Plate can reuse usual foods without asking the matcher. A separate food-naming call uses the parser model when applicable. ## Setup chat drove the first day of spend

The first approximately 23 hours of app telemetry, from November 23 at 20:22 to November 24 at 19:16 Sofia time, contained **35 calls costing $0.06904**. Four calls matched closely in time to admin test events. Excluding those likely checks leaves **31 calls costing $0.06236**, including eight Carbon setup turns that cost **$0.04417**, or **$0.00552 per turn** on average. The nine recorded Carbon setup calls had a 6.26 s median and a 12.67 s maximum. At the observed mix, parser, matcher and naming together cost about **$0.52 per 1,000 parser calls**, rising to **$1.48** with the two observed estimator calls included. The period is too short to project a monthly bill. The recorder captures signed-in requests, including admin checks and retries, and reports transport failures. Its counts exclude unsigned CLI benchmarks, and a successful HTTP response can still contain unusable output. [Production usage and attribution](../research_notes/Plate%20model%20lineup%20review/usage.md) · [Usage recorder](../src/Plate.Web/Integrations/AiUsageRecorder.cs)

Setup chat collects a profile across several turns, and Plate validates the fields and computes targets. Compare **Flash 3.8 low** against Carbon on the same complete Bulgarian and English transcripts. Check corrected answers, units, activity classification, follow-up questions and turns to completion. Flash costs about 63% less per token at today’s standard endpoint quotes with equal billed token counts, and the measured benefit should include whole-flow latency. Carbon remains defensible for an occasional setup if it materially improves that flow. ## Weekly data policy matters more than the price difference

OpenRouter says **Meta may use Contributor prompts and outputs to improve its products**. Plate’s weekly prompt includes calorie and macro averages, weight trends, goals, workout results and custom exercise names. The app normally generates one stored summary per eligible account per week. With 1,000 billed input and 200 billed output tokens held constant, GPT-6 Luna at **$0.10 / $0.50** adds **$0.00006 per generation**, about **$0.003 per user per year** at 52 generations. Luna has a listed zero-data-retention endpoint. Verify the actual routing and account or request controls, then test both languages for faithful numbers and sensible handling of missing data. Weekly summary quality has no comparable Plate test yet. ## Labels and estimates need known answers

**Keep Flash 3.8 low for labels during a direct comparison with Flash Lite**, which costs **$0.30 / $2.50**. In the 90-day production history, Flash 3.8 had six scans, one read error, and edits on all three saved scans. Review the edits against the packages if the photos are available. Use 20–30 transcribed Bulgarian and other EU labels, including glare, multi-photo inputs, decimal commas and per-100 g, per-100 ml and per-serving tables. Score exact fields, wrong units, unreadable fields and cost per usable scan. [Production label outcomes](../research_notes/Plate%20model%20lineup%20review/usage.md) · [Label spec](../SPEC.md#66-label-reading) · [Flash Lite pricing](https://openrouter.ai/google/gemini-3.5-flash-lite)

**Carbon medium has the weakest demonstrated return on its premium.** It handles unmatched rows in one call, supplies nutrition per 100 g and leaves portion size to the parser. Two recorded calls took **7.65 s and 11.45 s**. At the 12-second deadline or on invalid values, Plate keeps the parser’s estimates. Compare Carbon medium with Carbon low, Flash 3.8 low and the parser-only baseline using fixed portions and foods with independently known labels or weighed recipes. Score energy and macro error and bias, timeouts and usable results. Carbon medium earns its place if it reduces those errors enough to justify the extra wait and cost.
