# Plate usage and quality evidence

## How strong are the core food logging picks?

### Takeaway

Keep Muse Ember minimal and Gemini Decision 1. Each led Plate's closest available app-specific benchmark, with the combination costing roughly half a dollar per 1,000 requests for those two stages at the measured call mix when both run, before naming or optional estimation.

### Cited Findings

- Nov 21 parser benchmark: 62 texts, 80 expected items, 2 repeats with fixed Gemini Decision 1 matcher and no account history. Muse Ember (lowest effort, the unsuffixed run) led strict fully-correct text among 23 variants, 83.1%, 100% found, 100% amounts on checked items, 94.6% flags, zero failed, p50 2.174 s and p95 3.938 s, $0.000231 per parser call. End-to-end meal error mean 60 kcal and median 16 kcal by those references. Measured $0.231 per 1,000 parser calls. GPT-6 Luna lowest had 76.6%, 96.3% found, 99.4% amounts, 4.107 s p95, $0.094 per 1,000. DeepSeek V4 Flash had 75.8%, $0.045 per 1,000. Lower scenario cost is only $0.137–$0.186 per 1,000 parser calls. Strongest runner-up strict accuracy GPT-6.1 Terra none at 80.6%, $0.910 per 1,000, 6.189 s p95. Ember and Luna list the same rates but differed in average billed output (406 vs 162). The benchmark's single-piece ground truth conflicted with the app rule, one model's schema was broken at the time, Qwen/Tencent timeouts and matcher contention confound some comparisons. Do not treat parser score as production accuracy or output tokens as the only influence on cost. [Parser results](../../reports/parsebench-2026-11-21.txt:1) [Benchmark effort](../../src/Plate.Web/Features/Feedback/ParseBenchmark.cs:75)
- Nov 21 matcher benchmark: 96 fixed parser entries/107 items over three repeats, 20 decision models plus off, ten true catalogue absences and two retrieval misses. Gemini Decision 1 got 98.1% final including Plate's confidence/fallback rules, 100% BG, 95.6% English, 100% correct rejection on absences, 100% repeat agreement, p50 320 ms/p95 451 ms, no failures or >3 s, $0.0823 benchmark total (~$0.286 per 1,000 benchmark entries, 96 x 3). Ranking off had 76.6%. TypeSafe Jev 1.14 had 95.3%, p95 261 ms, $0.0499 total (~$0.173 per 1,000 entries); Microsoft Decision 1.1 95.3%, p95 417 ms, $0.0333 (~$0.116); Perplexity PPLX Decider v1.3 Free 93.8%, 70% rejection on absences, p95 879 ms. [Matcher report](../../reports/matchbench-2026-11-21.txt:1)
- Nov 22 local seeded ranking-only benchmark improved 84/107 to 97/107 on development and 33/39 to 34/39 on held-out food families after identity/variant ranking changes. Do not compare Nov 21 production catalog baseline directly to latest local ranking on a different snapshot. The paid matcher has not been re-run head-to-head on the current ranking/held-out set in the checked-in reports. [Matcher README](../../tools/matchbench/README.md:1)
- Parser extracts food identity, amounts and state, then Plate checks personal usual foods/own matches and looks in its catalog. Decision API is invoked at most once per parse when needed, and silently drops to deterministic ranking after 3 s. Applying a usual food can avoid asking a matchup question. The separate namer uses the parser model for eligible matched rows and has a fallback. Benchmark parser cost excludes namer and downstream matcher/estimator. [Specification](../../SPEC.md:396) [Matching and naming](../../SPEC.md:414)
- Historical November 21 30-day digest showed 8 parses, 7 saves, 4 corrected runs, 6 negative rating events and zero label reads. Multiple rating events can apply to the same run. This is an old snapshot and too small for model accuracy or current spend extrapolation. The weighed 40-meal plan is documented with no checked-in results. [Prior report](../../reports/Improving%20food%20logging%20accuracy.md:5) [Pilot](../../reports/weighed-meal-pilot.md:1)

### Inferences

- Keep both defaults in the image. Use GPT-6 Luna lowest as a compatible rollback candidate. If matcher spend matters later, compare current Gemini with Jev 1.14 and stronger ranking off on held-out cases. Benchmark headline is not a justification to spend less at current volume or remove the matcher.

### Gaps

- No billed workload or production request was queried for this component. November 21 local reports lack confidence intervals and do not establish real-world accuracy.

## How should the lower-volume roles be configured?

### Takeaway

The most defensible change is moving weekly summaries off Contributor because they contain private nutrition, training and weight facts. Keep setup chat Carbon low and Flash 3.8 low temporarily if they work. The first worthwhile cost/latency comparison is estimator Carbon medium versus Carbon low and Flash 3.8 low on unmatched foods with known references, with the parser-only estimate as baseline.

### Cited Findings

- Setup chat runs at first sign-in or on manual rerun, sends profile draft and last 20 turns, validates captured fields server-side, computes targets in code, and offers a form fallback. Carbon low makes sense if it keeps Bulgarian conversation and extraction reliable. Cheaper Flash 3.8 low or GPT-6 Luna lowest are candidates only after testing multi-turn BG/EN flows, correctly extracting units, handling corrections, classifying activity, and asking for unknowns. Avoid a daily-volume claim for onboarding. [Specification](../../SPEC.md:459)
- Weekly summary is cached, normally one success per eligible user/week after Today is visited, with possible retries/language change. Sends no name/email/notes, but the payload contains goal and rate, macro averages/targets, weight trend, workout/strength records and exercise names including custom ones. Core computes facts and the model writes the prose. OpenRouter explicitly says Contributor inputs and outputs may be used to improve Meta products, and its endpoint catalog lists only Meta. A $0.10/$0.50 model over the $0.10/$0.20 Contributor tier costs an extra $0.00006 per summary at 200 output tokens (same input rate; held token counts), close to $0.00026/user per average month. GPT-6 Luna lowest is a reasonable first fit and has structured output and compatible available endpoints, but no weekly-summary quality comparison. Muse Ember minimal same sticker rates and structured output, check actual endpoint's data treatment. Test week with missing nutrition/weight fields, both languages, fidelity of numbers and safe guidance. [Summary spec](../../SPEC.md:475) [Payload code](../../src/Plate.Web/Integrations/OpenRouterWeeklySummaryWriter.cs:135) [Contributor model card](https://openrouter.ai/meta/muse-spark-1.3-contributor/providers) [GPT-6 Luna](https://openrouter.ai/openai/gpt-6-luna/providers) [Provider settings](https://github.com/OpenRouterTeam/docs/blob/main/guides/routing/provider-selection.mdx)
- Label reading is visual structured transcription of up to three photographs of a single package. Plate prefers per 100 g/ml, preserves basis, converts kJ/per-serving in code, asks user to verify and stores edits. Public vision scores cannot measure Plate's exact Bulgarian field/basis fidelity. Keep Flash 3.8 low until testing against cheapest viable vision option e.g. Muse Ember minimal or Gemini Flash-Lite if current catalog and privacy fit. 20–30 human-transcribed Bulgarian/EU labels covering decimal commas, kJ/kcal and g/ml/serving bases, poor lighting, glare, multiphoto and no-label cases. Count exact core fields and wrong bases, null-vs-invented data; measure p95, retries and cost per usable scan. The bundled smoke only checks kcal on a single image. [Label spec](../../SPEC.md:489) [Label smoke test](../../src/Plate.Web/Integrations/OpenRouterLabelReader.cs:62) [Ember vision and API](https://openrouter.ai/meta/muse-ember)
- Food estimator gets one request for low-confidence unmatched rows, also used for unmatched recipe ingredients. Its estimates are per 100 g, it gets nearby candidates as references, and it can't improve the parser's estimated grams. Over 12 s or if invalid, parser estimates survive. The built-in smoke checks only plausible values on one Lidl pastry, not accuracy. Carbon medium makes sense as an occasional quality-first fallback but quality premium is unverified. Compare Carbon medium, Carbon low, Flash 3.8 low, and current parser guesses using weighed recipes or known labels hidden from the matcher, with portions fixed for scoring. Score kcal/protein/carbs/fat mean absolute error and bias, schema/timeout, plus end-to-end time. Without ingredient quantities or label, even stronger model's answer is an estimate. [Estimator spec](../../SPEC.md:411) [Estimator implementation](../../src/Plate.Web/Integrations/OpenRouterFoodEstimator.cs:16) [Smoke semantics](../../src/Plate.Web/Integrations/OpenRouterFoodEstimator.cs:64)
- Reported spend is tracked per account and task including namer. Signed-in OpenRouter POSTs only, cost from OpenRouter usage if present, and bad HTTP responses count as failures. CLI tests/eval excluded from this ledger and HTTP-success schema failures can still show as successful in usage recorder. 'AI total' adds costs with unknown amounts counted as zero, so mention coverage start and missing values. [Usage recorder](../../src/Plate.Web/Integrations/AiUsageRecorder.cs:8) [CLI](../../src/Plate.Web/Cli/FeedbackCommand.cs:532)

### Inferences

- Model policy matters disproportionately for weekly summary and setup, since latter carries additional personal demographics. Recommend a model/provider whose request policy meets the user's expectations; zero-data-retention is separate from simply changing model names. Do not assume the OpenRouter account settings.
- Compare estimates before changing label reader or onboarding on sticker prices; estimator has highest listed rates and its latency is on the review path, but actual usage may make spend negligible. Do not switch based on numbers rounded to cents or read a couple of estimates as a benchmark.

### Gaps

- No checked-in comparative eval for Carbon or Flash 3.8 in these workflows. Confirm capture at the actual configured effort and provider. Privacy conditions differ per endpoint; OpenRouter describes coverage, but its model card does not establish the account's choices.
